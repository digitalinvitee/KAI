## English

### The concept

KAI is a fictional omakase restaurant: a nine-metre counter of hinoki cypress with ten seats
and a quiet dining room behind it. The name comes from 会 (kai), a meeting or gathering. The
same character ends ichigo ichie (一期一会), the tea-ceremony idea that every encounter
happens only once. That idea shaped the site: one evening, one counter, one conversation,
and a design that leaves out everything that does not serve it. Brand line: “The art of less.”

### Art direction

Japanese architectural minimalism meets European editorial design. The palette stays with
natural materials: warm ivory, charcoal, stone, sand and a single burnt-rust accent. The page
alternates between light editorial sections and a dark, cinematic canvas: when the guest
reaches "The Space", the whole page dims like a dining room at the start of service.

Typography carries the identity in a single typeface: Manrope, set upright in four
weights. Light carries the KAI wordmark, spaced wide like lettering on a wall, and the large
headlines; Regular the reading text; Medium the navigation, buttons and small uppercase
labels that work like architectural annotations: figure numbers, times, materials. There
are no italics and no decorative serifs. The one Japanese character is set in Noto Sans JP,
whose even strokes match the Latin.

No cherry blossoms, no rising suns, no decorative Japanese characters. Cultural references
are few, accurate and explained.

The imagery is a set of AI-generated concept visuals in one consistent language: low,
warm light, pale hinoki and dark stone, a garden seen through glass, and close detail. They depict the fictional
restaurant and are labelled as AI-generated on the site.

### The digital experience

- **Hero:** one full-bleed image, the KAI wordmark at architectural scale and one
  sentence. The image settles as the light comes up; the letters rise out of masks. One
  clear action, "Reserve a table", with a quieter route to the menu and the opening time
  beside it.
- **Philosophy:** asymmetric editorial composition with three principles (season,
  precision, hospitality) and the story behind the name.
- **Menu:** an editorial carte instead of product cards. Omakase, à la carte and drinks
  switch instantly from a category bar that stays in view on a phone; EU allergens appear
  for every dish at the touch of a switch; prices follow the reader's language (`€185` /
  `185 €`) and are labelled as a sample. The whole menu lives in one file that the
  restaurant can edit, in both languages side by side.
- **The Space:** an asymmetric architectural gallery with figure captions, a materials index
  and controlled parallax.
- **Private dining:** a quiet invitation for private dinners, celebrations and business
  evenings, with an enquiry that prepares an email or WhatsApp message. It promises no
  packages or prices that the fictional restaurant has not defined.
- **Reservations:** a three-step booking flow (table, details, review) with a real
  calendar, validation in both languages and three ways of working: the portfolio demo
  with simulated availability; a request the guest sends by WhatsApp or email, for
  restaurants without a booking system; or a hand-over to the restaurant's existing
  booking provider. None needs a backend, and the demo is labelled as a demo at every step.
- **Visit:** address, directions, opening hours, call, WhatsApp and email in one place,
  driven by a settings file. On phones a Menu · Reserve bar stays within thumb reach.
- **Demo dashboard:** the restaurant's side. Daily overview, seat occupancy per start time,
  filters, search, booking details, cancellation. A booking made on the website appears
  here instantly.

### Key interactions

Orchestrated hero entrance · line-by-line headline reveals · images that open from below ·
canvas cross-fade into the dark section · sliding menu indicator with keyboard support ·
calendar with arrow-key navigation · availability check with seat counts · a category bar
that follows the reader · allergens on demand · a switch between three booking modes · an
enquiry dialog that writes the message for the guest · language switch that keeps the
reader's place and every booking selection.

### Responsive and accessible

Designed mobile-first and checked at 320, 375, 390, 768, 1024 and 1440 px: art-directed
hero crop for phones, a full-screen menu, a sticky booking action bar and a stacked
dashboard. Semantic HTML, visible focus, keyboard-operable tabs and calendar, labelled forms
with clear errors, screen-reader announcements and full `prefers-reduced-motion` support.

### Technology

HTML, modern CSS (registered custom properties, container queries), vanilla JavaScript
modules, GSAP 3 with ScrollTrigger, esbuild for production, a zero-dependency localisation
engine (EN/DE, ready for more languages) and a frontend demo reservation engine with unit
tests for capacity rules and duplicate protection. Static hosting on GitHub Pages.

### What it shows a restaurant owner

A website that feels like the restaurant: calm, precise, bilingual from day one, with a
menu guests can actually read on a phone and a booking flow that respects their time. And
one that fits how the restaurant already works: bookings arrive through the system or the
WhatsApp and email they already use, group enquiries have a place to go, the opening
hours and contact details are always one tap away, and the team can change a dish or a
price without calling the agency.

### Das Konzept

KAI ist ein fiktives Omakase-Restaurant: ein neun Meter langer Tresen aus Hinoki-Zypresse mit
zehn Plätzen und ein ruhiger Gastraum dahinter. Der Name steht für 会 (kai), Begegnung.
Dasselbe Zeichen beschließt ichigo ichie (一期一会), den Gedanken aus der Teezeremonie,
dass jede Begegnung nur ein einziges Mal geschieht. Daraus entstand die Website: ein Abend,
ein Tresen, ein Gespräch – und eine Gestaltung, die alles weglässt, was nicht dient.
Leitsatz: „Die Kunst des Weglassens.“

### Art Direction

Japanischer Architektur-Minimalismus trifft europäisches Editorial Design. Die Farben
bleiben bei natürlichen Materialien: warmes Elfenbein, Anthrazit, Stein, Sand und ein
einziger rostroter Akzent. Helle editoriale Abschnitte wechseln mit einer dunklen,
filmischen Fläche: Sobald der Gast „Der Raum“ erreicht, dimmt die ganze Seite wie ein
Gastraum zu Beginn des Service.

Die Typografie trägt die Identität mit einer einzigen Schrift: Manrope, aufrecht in vier
Schnitten. Light für die weit gesperrte Wortmarke und die großen Headlines, Regular für den
Lesetext, Medium für Navigation, Buttons und kleine Versalien-Labels, die wie
architektonische Annotationen wirken. Keine Kursiven, keine dekorativen Serifen. Das
japanische Zeichen steht in der Noto Sans JP, deren gleichmäßige Striche zur lateinischen
Schrift passen.

### Das digitale Erlebnis

Die Bildwelt besteht aus KI-generierten Konzeptvisualisierungen in einer einheitlichen
Sprache: tiefes, warmes Licht, helles Hinoki und dunkler Stein, ein Garten hinter Glas und
nahe Details. Sie sind auf
der Website als KI-generiert gekennzeichnet.

Hero mit einem einzigen Bild, inszeniertem Auftakt und einer klaren Handlung („Tisch
reservieren“) · editoriale Philosophie · Speisekarte mit mitlaufender Kategorienleiste,
EU-Allergenen auf Knopfdruck und sprachabhängigen Preisen, gepflegt in einer einzigen Datei ·
architektonische Galerie · Private Dining mit einer Anfrage, die eine E-Mail oder
WhatsApp-Nachricht vorbereitet · dreistufige Reservierung in drei Betriebsarten (Demo,
Anfrage per WhatsApp oder E-Mail, Weiterleitung zum bestehenden Reservierungssystem), ganz
ohne Backend · Anfahrt, Öffnungszeiten und Kontakt an einem Ort · auf dem Smartphone eine
Leiste mit Speisekarte und Reservierung in Daumennähe · Demo-Dashboard mit Tagesübersicht,
Belegung, Filtern, Suche und Stornierung. Alles zweisprachig, mobil zuerst gestaltet und
barrierearm umgesetzt.

### Technologie

HTML, modernes CSS, Vanilla JavaScript, GSAP 3 mit ScrollTrigger, esbuild, eigene
Lokalisierung (EN/DE, erweiterbar) und eine Demo-Reservierungslogik mit Unit-Tests.
Statisches Hosting über GitHub Pages.
